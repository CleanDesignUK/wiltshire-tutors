GCSE Maths Revision for Wiltshire Students: A Practical Plan for Years 10 and 11

A sensible way to revise GCSE Maths, identify knowledge gaps and use past papers without wasting time.

By Wiltshire Tutoring Team · 12 November 2026

A sensible way to revise GCSE Maths, identify knowledge gaps and use past papers without wasting time.

Start with the exact GCSE specification

First confirm whether the student is studying AQA, Pearson Edexcel, OCR or another awarding body, and which tier they are likely to sit. The biggest gains often come from revising skills that underpin many other topics, not simply attempting the hardest questions available.

A short topic audit can separate work the student can complete confidently from work that still needs an explanation. Use marked school assessments to identify repeated errors rather than relying on a vague feeling that Maths is difficult.

Revise by topic before mixing questions

Build a weekly routine containing a few core tasks: quick arithmetic or algebra retrieval, one focused topic, then a handful of exam-style questions. Fractions, percentages, rearranging formulae, graphs and proportional reasoning have wide application.

When a question goes wrong, record why. Did the student misread information, choose an unsuitable method, make an arithmetic slip or lack a key concept? Each of these requires a different response.

Use past papers as feedback, not just a score

Past papers are useful when students review them closely. After marking, reattempt incorrect questions with no model answer visible, then try a similar question on a later day. Under timed conditions, students also practise deciding when to move on.

For Foundation and Higher tier preparation alike, show full working and check that calculator technique is secure. A well-organised record of errors can become a personalised revision checklist.

How online tutoring can help

A GCSE Maths tutor can diagnose weak prerequisite skills, model methods and gradually reduce prompts until the student can work independently. At Wiltshire Tutoring, lessons are matched to the qualification and targets; no fixed course or subscription is required.
